    by Jane Austen

    1813 · Novel · Romance

    Elizabeth Bennet and Mr. Darcy misjudge each other in a comedy of marriage, income, and pride. Austen’s second published novel is still the surest entrance to her shelf.

    Jane Eyre

    Jane Eyre

    by Charlotte Brontë

    1847 · Novel · Gothic · Romance

    An orphan becomes a governess and refuses every arrangement that would make her less than a person. The house at Thornfield holds a secret the marriage plot cannot step around.

    Frankenstein

    by Mary Shelley

    1818 · Novel · Gothic · Science fiction

    Victor Frankenstein builds a living being and then abandons it. The creature’s education, and Victor’s refusal to answer for what he made, are the book’s real experiment.

    Dracula

    Dracula

    by Bram Stoker

    1897 · Novel · Gothic

    Jonathan Harker’s travel diary opens onto a count who intends to move to London. The rest of the novel is a coalition of notes, blood, and timetables trying to stop him.

    The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

    by Arthur Conan Doyle

    1892 · Short stories · Mystery

    Twelve early cases, from a speckled band to a scandal in Bohemia, narrated by Dr. Watson. This is the collection that fixed Holmes in the popular imagination.

    Moby-Dick

    by Herman Melville

    1851 · Novel · Adventure

    Ishmael ships on the Pequod under a captain hunting one white whale. The book stops for whales, work, and metaphysics, then returns to the chase.

    by Mark Twain

    1884 · Novel · Adventure

    Huck Finn fakes his death and rafts the Mississippi with Jim, who is escaping enslavement. Twain’s novel is funny, and it does not let the joke forgive the country it travels.

    by Charles Dickens

    Pip is lifted out of a forge by money whose source he misunderstands. Dickens’s late novel is about class, gratitude, and the patron Pip invents in his head.

    by Thomas Carlyle

    1867 · History
