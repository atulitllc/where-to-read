The shelf

Pride and Prejudice

by Jane Austen

1813 · Novel · Romance

Elizabeth Bennet and Mr. Darcy misjudge each other in a comedy of marriage, income, and pride. Austen’s second published novel is still the surest entrance to her shelf.

Emma

by Jane Austen

1815 · Novel · Romance

Emma Woodhouse has money, leisure, and a talent for arranging other people’s lives. The novel lets her be charming and wrong in the same sentence.

Sense and Sensibility

by Jane Austen

1811 · Novel · Romance

Two sisters, turned out of their home, try sense and feeling as ways of surviving love and reduced means. It was Austen’s first published novel.

Persuasion

by Jane Austen

1817 · Novel · Romance

Anne Elliot broke an engagement years ago and has had to live quietly with the decision. A return of the man she refused gives the book its second chance.

Jane Eyre

by Charlotte Brontë

1847 · Novel · Gothic · Romance

An orphan becomes a governess and refuses every arrangement that would make her less than a person. The house at Thornfield holds a secret the marriage plot cannot step around.

Wuthering Heights

by Emily Brontë

1847 · Novel · Gothic

Heathcliff and Catherine’s attachment wrecks two households across two generations. The story is told by a tenant and a housekeeper, never by the pair at its center.

Frankenstein

by Mary Shelley

1818 · Novel · Gothic · Science fiction

Victor Frankenstein builds a living being and then abandons it. The creature’s education, and Victor’s refusal to answer for what he made, are the book’s real experiment.

Dracula

by Bram Stoker

1897 · Novel · Gothic

Jonathan Harker’s travel diary opens onto a count who intends to move to London. The rest of the novel is a coalition of notes, blood, and timetables trying to stop him.

The Picture of Dorian Gray

by Oscar Wilde

1890 · Novel · Gothic

A young man wishes that a portrait would age in his place. Wilde’s only novel follows the wish into influence, cruelty, and a picture locked in a room.

The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

by Arthur Conan Doyle

1892 · Short stories · Mystery

Twelve early cases, from a speckled band to a scandal in Bohemia, narrated by Dr. Watson. This is the collection that fixed Holmes in the popular imagination.

The Hound of the Baskervilles

by Arthur Conan Doyle

1902 · Novel · Mystery · Gothic

A family legend about a hound on the Devon moor brings Holmes out of a silence Doyle had meant to be permanent. The rational explanation has to earn its place against the fog.

Alice's Adventures in Wonderland

by Lewis Carroll

1865 · Children's books

Alice follows a rabbit and finds a world that argues with her about size, manners, and the meaning of words. Carroll wrote it for a real child and then built a classic out of the joke.

The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

by L. Frank Baum

1900 · Children's books · Adventure

A cyclone carries Dorothy from Kansas to a road of yellow brick. The companions she picks up all want something a wizard in a city claims he can grant.

Peter and Wendy

by J. M. Barrie

1911 · Children's books · Adventure

The Darling children leave the nursery for Neverland with a boy who refuses the future. Barrie’s novel keeps the flying, and also the cost of never coming home.

Treasure Island

by Robert Louis Stevenson

1883 · Adventure · Novel

Jim Hawkins finds a map and goes to sea with a crew that is not what the advertisement promised. Long John Silver remains the model of a charming villain.

The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

by Robert Louis Stevenson

1886 · Gothic · Novel

A London lawyer tries to understand the tie between a respectable doctor and a violent man who shares his house. Stevenson’s short novel is a study of a self split on purpose.

A Christmas Carol

by Charles Dickens

1843 · Short stories

Ebenezer Scrooge is walked through his past, the present he refuses to see, and a future he can still change. Dickens wrote it quickly, and it became the Christmas story people mean.

A Tale of Two Cities

by Charles Dickens

1859 · Novel

London and Paris before and during the French Revolution, joined by a look-alike, a prisoner, and a sacrifice. The opening sentence is famous; the plot earns it.

Great Expectations

by Charles Dickens

1861 · Novel

Pip is lifted out of a forge by money whose source he misunderstands. Dickens’s late novel is about class, gratitude, and the patron Pip invents in his head.

Moby-Dick

by Herman Melville

1851 · Novel · Adventure

Ishmael ships on the Pequod under a captain hunting one white whale. The book stops for whales, work, and metaphysics, then returns to the chase.

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn

by Mark Twain

1884 · Novel · Adventure

Huck Finn fakes his death and rafts the Mississippi with Jim, who is escaping enslavement. Twain’s novel is funny, and it does not let the joke forgive the country it travels.

Little Women

by Louisa May Alcott

1868 · Novel

Meg, Jo, Beth, and Amy March grow up in a household short of money and long on opinions. Jo’s wish to write is as much the plot as any marriage.

Anne of Green Gables

by L. M. Montgomery

1908 · Novel · Children's books

Matthew and Marilla Cuthbert ask for a boy from the orphanage and receive Anne Shirley. She talks, imagines, and slowly makes herself necessary to Avonlea.

The Secret Garden

by Frances Hodgson Burnett

1911 · Children's books · Novel

Mary Lennox arrives at a Yorkshire manor and finds a garden no one is allowed to enter. Tending it changes her, and the cousin shut in another room.

The Call of the Wild

by Jack London

1903 · Adventure · Novel

Buck, a stolen California dog, is worked north into the Yukon. London tells the story from the animal’s side of the harness.

The Time Machine

by H. G. Wells

1895 · Science fiction · Novel

A Victorian inventor rides his machine to a future in which humanity has split. Wells’s novella is a social argument dressed as a traveler’s report.

The War of the Worlds

by H. G. Wells

1898 · Science fiction · Novel

Martian cylinders fall on the English countryside and the narrator walks through the collapse of ordinary life. Wells aimed the invasion at the empire that had invaded others.

The Yellow Wallpaper

by Charlotte Perkins Gilman

1892 · Short stories · Gothic

A woman confined to a room for her health begins to watch the pattern in the wallpaper. Gilman’s story is brief and does not offer the cure the physician promised.

The Awakening

by Kate Chopin

1899 · Novel

Edna Pontellier, a wife in Creole New Orleans, stops agreeing that her life is already decided. Chopin’s novel was received coldly and later read as a classic.

Heart of Darkness

by Joseph Conrad

1899 · Short stories · Adventure

Marlow tells a deck audience about his trip upriver to find Kurtz, an ivory agent who has become a rumor. Conrad’s tale is about empire and about storytelling.

The Metamorphosis

by Franz Kafka

1915 · Short stories

Gregor Samsa wakes as an insect and worries first about missing the train. The household’s patience, and then its accounting, are the rest of the story. The link is to a public-domain English translation.

Crime and Punishment

by Fyodor Dostoevsky

1866 · Novel

Raskolnikov tests a theory that some people may kill for a higher purpose, and the theory does not survive the act. This entry points to a public-domain English translation.

Anna Karenina

by Leo Tolstoy

1877 · Novel · Romance

Anna’s affair and Levin’s search for a workable life run side by side in imperial Russia. Tolstoy gives the social world as much weight as the passion. The link is to a public-domain English translation.

The Count of Monte Cristo

by Alexandre Dumas

1844 · Novel · Adventure

Edmond Dantès is imprisoned on a false charge and comes back, years later, with a new name and a plan. Dumas makes patience itself into an adventure.

Les Misérables

by Victor Hugo

1862 · Novel

Jean Valjean leaves prison and is pursued across France by Inspector Javert, while Hugo pauses to write about sewers, convents, and justice. The catalog links a public-domain English translation.

Don Quixote

by Miguel de Cervantes

1605 · Novel · Adventure

A country gentleman renames himself a knight and rides out to enforce the plots of chivalric books. Cervantes’s novel is the comedy of taking reading literally. The link is to a public-domain English translation.

The Odyssey

by Homer

Adventure

After the war at Troy, Odysseus tries to get home while suitors occupy his house. This catalog links Samuel Butler’s public-domain prose translation, not a modern verse one.

Meditations

by Marcus Aurelius

Philosophy

Private notes on judgment, death, and the discipline of wanting less than circumstances offer. The English text here is a public-domain translation of the emperor’s Greek.

The Prince

by Niccolò Machiavelli

1532 · Philosophy

A short treatise on acquiring and holding a state, written in exile and dedicated to a Medici. Machiavelli prefers what rulers do to what mirror-books said they should do.

Walden

by Henry David Thoreau

1854 · Memoir · Philosophy

Thoreau’s account of two years in a cabin by Walden Pond, arranged by season and by argument. It is a book about economy in the old sense: how a life is furnished.

Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass

by Frederick Douglass

1845 · Memoir

Douglass describes enslavement in Maryland, the fight to read, and his escape. He published the Narrative to answer readers who claimed no one in bondage could have written it.

The Souls of Black Folk

by W. E. B. Du Bois

1903 · Memoir

Essays on reconstruction, education, music, and what Du Bois named double consciousness. The book argues, and it also remembers.

The Scarlet Letter

by Nathaniel Hawthorne

1850 · Novel · Gothic

Hester Prynne wears a letter the town requires, while the father of her child stays unnamed. Hawthorne’s novel is about punishment as a public art.

Dubliners

by James Joyce

1914 · Short stories

Fifteen stories of Dublin life, ending with The Dead. Joyce called the method a kind of clarity; the characters often stop just short of changing.

The Great Gatsby

by F. Scott Fitzgerald

1925 · Novel

Nick Carraway rents a house next to Jay Gatsby’s parties and slowly learns what the parties are for. Fitzgerald’s novel entered the public domain in the United States in 2021.
