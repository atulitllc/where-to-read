Abraham Lincoln's First Inaugural Address

Abraham Lincoln's First Inaugural Address

by Abraham Lincoln

History · Letters

Abraham Lincoln's first inaugural address was delivered as part of his taking of the oath of office for his first term as the sixteenth president of the United States. The speech, delivered at the United States Capitol, was primarily addressed to the people of the South and was intended to succinctly state Lincoln's intended policies and desires toward that section, where seven states had seceded from the Union and formed the Confederate States of America.

Common Sense

Common Sense

by Thomas Paine

History · Science · Letters

Writing in clear and persuasive prose, Paine collected moral and political arguments to encourage common people in the Colonies to fight for egalitarian government. Common Sense was published anonymously at the beginning of the American Revolution and became an immediate sensation.

Daddy-Long-Legs was initially serialized in the April–September issues of the Ladies' Home Journal, and first published in book form by The Century Company.

De Profundis

De Profundis

by Oscar Wilde

Letters

De Profundis (Latin: "from the depths") is a letter written by Oscar Wilde during his imprisonment in Reading Gaol, to his friend and lover Lord Alfred "Bosie" Douglas. In its first half, Wilde recounts their previous relationship and extravagant lifestyle which resulted eventually in Wilde's conviction and imprisonment for gross indecency.

Fenimore Cooper's Literary Offences

Fenimore Cooper's Literary Offences

by Mark Twain

History · Humor · Letters

Fenimore Cooper's Literary Offences draws on examples from The Deerslayer and The Pathfinder from Cooper's Leatherstocking Tales. The essay is characteristic of Twain's biting, derisive, and highly satirical style of literary criticism, a form he also used to deride such authors as Oliver Goldsmith, George Eliot, Jane Austen, and Robert Louis Stevenson.

Culture and Anarchy

Culture and Anarchy

by Matthew Arnold

Philosophy · Letters

Culture and Anarchy: An Essay in Political and Social Criticism is a series of periodical essays by Matthew Arnold, first published in Cornhill Magazine –68 and collected as a book. Arnold's famous piece of writing on culture established his High Victorian cultural agenda which remained dominant in debate from the 1860s until the 1950s.

An Essay on Criticism

An Essay on Criticism

by Alexander Pope

Poetry · Essays · Letters

An Essay on Criticism is one of the first major poems written by the English writer Alexander Pope (1688–1744), published. An Essay on Criticism is the source of the famous quotations "To err is human; to forgive, divine", "A little learning is a dang'rous thing" (frequently paraphrased as "A little knowledge is a dangerous thing"), and "Fools rush in where angels fear to tread".

by John Donne

Philosophy · Religion · Letters

Devotions Upon Emergent Occasions; Together with Death's Duel covers death, rebirth and the early modern concept of sickness as a visit from God, reflecting internal sinfulness. The Red Man's Rebuke

The Red Man's Rebuke

by Simon Pokagon

History · Letters

The Red Man's Rebuke, later titled The Red Man's Greeting, was an essay written by Simon Pokagon (Potowatomi, c. 1830–1899) which he distributed to those attending the World's Columbian Exposition. The Red Man's Rebuke was published in the form of a sixteen-page, palm-sized birch bark booklet, of which an unknown number of copies were distributed.
