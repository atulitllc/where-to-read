Subject

Children's books

Tales long passed to young readers, from nursery classics to schoolroom adventures. The catalog itself is written for a general audience looking up a public-domain text.

Alice's Adventures in Wonderland

Alice's Adventures in Wonderland

by Lewis Carroll

1865 · Children's books

Alice follows a rabbit and finds a world that argues with her about size, manners, and the meaning of words. Carroll wrote it for a real child and then built a classic out of the joke.

The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

by L. Frank Baum

1900 · Children's books · Adventure

A cyclone carries Dorothy from Kansas to a road of yellow brick. The companions she picks up all want something a wizard in a city claims he can grant.

Peter and Wendy

Peter and Wendy

by J. M. Barrie

1911 · Children's books · Adventure

The Darling children leave the nursery for Neverland with a boy who refuses the future. Barrie’s novel keeps the flying, and also the cost of never coming home.

Anne of Green Gables

Anne of Green Gables

by L. M. Montgomery

1908 · Novel · Children's books

Matthew and Marilla Cuthbert ask for a boy from the orphanage and receive Anne Shirley. She talks, imagines, and slowly makes herself necessary to Avonlea.

The Secret Garden

The Secret Garden

by Frances Hodgson Burnett

1911 · Children's books · Novel

Mary Lennox arrives at a Yorkshire manor and finds a garden no one is allowed to enter. Tending it changes her, and the cousin shut in another room.

Through the Looking-Glass

Through the Looking-Glass

by Lewis Carroll

Fantasy · Children's books · Novel

Through the Looking-Glass is the sequel to his Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, in which many of the characters were anthropomorphic playing cards. In this second novel the theme is chess.

The Hunting of the Snark: An Agony in Eight Fits

The Hunting of the Snark: An Agony in Eight Fits

by Lewis Carroll

Poetry · Children's books · Humor

The Hunting of the Snark: An Agony in Eight Fits is typically categorised as a nonsense poem. It borrows the setting, some creatures, and eight portmanteau words from Carroll's earlier poem "Jabberwocky" in his children's novel Through the Looking-Glass.

The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete

The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete

by Mark Twain

Adventure · Children's books · Novel

The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete is set in the 1830s–1840s in the fictional town of St. Petersburg, which is based on Hannibal, Missouri, where Twain lived as a boy. In the novel, Sawyer has several adventures, often with his friend Huckleberry Finn.

Tess of the d'Urbervilles: A Pure Woman

Tess of the d'Urbervilles: A Pure Woman

by Thomas Hardy

Children's books · Novel

Tess of the d'Urbervilles: A Pure Woman initially appeared in a censored and serialised version, published by the British illustrated newspaper The Graphic, then in book form in three volumes, and as a single volume. Although now considered a major novel of the 19th century, Tess of the d'Urbervilles received mixed reviews when it first appeared, in part because it challenged the morals of late Victorian England.

Charlotte Temple

Charlotte Temple

by Mrs. Rowson

1794 · Children's books · Novel

Charlotte Temple recounts a schoolgirl, Charlotte Temple, who is seduced by a British officer and brought to America, where she is abandoned, pregnant, sick and in poverty. The first American edition was published in 1794 and the novel became a bestseller.

At the Back of the North Wind

At the Back of the North Wind

by George MacDonald

Science fiction · Fantasy · Children's books

At the Back of the North Wind was serialized in the children's magazine Good Words for the Young beginning and was published in book form. At the Back of the North Wind is a fantasy centered on a boy named Diamond and his adventures with the North Wind.

Black Beauty

Black Beauty

by Anna Sewell

Children's books · History · Novel

Black Beauty was written from a horse as main character's perspective. She wrote it in the last years of her life, during which she was bedridden and seriously ill.

The Fortunes and Misfortunes of the Famous Moll Flanders

The Fortunes and Misfortunes of the Famous Moll Flanders

by Daniel Defoe

Adventure · Children's books · Novel

The Fortunes and Misfortunes of the Famous Moll Flanders purports to be an autobiographical account of the life of the eponymous heroine, detailing her exploits from birth until old age as she uses her wits, cunning and charm to provide herself with financial security. While the novel is now generally attributed to Defoe, and his name is given as the author in modern printings, the original text does not list an author and was presented as an autobiography.

Kidnapped

Kidnapped

by Robert Louis Stevenson

Adventure · Children's books · History

Kidnapped has attracted the praise and admiration of writers as diverse as Henry James, Jorge Luis Borges, and Hilary Mantel. Kidnapped is written in English with some dialogue in the Lowland Scots language.

Just David

Just David

by Eleanor H. (Eleanor Hodgman) Porter

Children's books · Novel

Just David was among the top six bestsellers in cities across the United States, and in July it was the second bestselling novel. Originally published by Houghton Mifflin, it recounts a young boy, David, who must learn to adapt to living with others after the death of his reclusive father; along the way, the villagers and his adoptive parents adapt as much or more to him.

Little Lord Fauntleroy

Little Lord Fauntleroy

by Frances Hodgson Burnett

Children's books · Novel

Little Lord Fauntleroy was published as a serial in St. Nicholas Magazine, then as a book by Scribner's (the publisher of St. Nicholas) in New York. Little Lord Fauntleroy was also published that year in November by Frederick Warne & Co.

Silas Marner

Silas Marner

by George Eliot

Children's books · Novel

Silas Marner: The Weaver of Raveloe is the third novel by English author George Eliot; it was published on 2 April. A seemingly simple story of a linen weaver, Silas Marner is notable for its strong realist bent, as well as its sophisticated treatment of a variety of issues ranging from religion to industrialisation to community.

Sylvie and Bruno

Sylvie and Bruno

by Lewis Carroll

Science fiction · Fantasy · Children's books

Sylvie and Bruno, first published, and its second volume Sylvie and Bruno Concluded published, form the last novel by Lewis Carroll published during his lifetime. Both volumes were illustrated by Harry Furniss.

The Light Princess

The Light Princess

by George MacDonald

Science fiction · Fantasy · Children's books

The Light Princess was published in as a story within the larger story Adela Cathcart. Drawing on inspiration from the story "Sleeping Beauty", it recounts a princess afflicted by a constant weightlessness, unable to get her feet on the ground, both literally and metaphorically, until she finds a love that brings her down to earth.

A Child's History of England

A Child's History of England

by Charles Dickens

Children's books · History

A Child's History of England first appeared in serial form in Household Words, running from 25 January to 10 December. Dickens also published the work in book form in three volumes: the first volume on 20 December, the second on 25 December and the third on 24 December.

The Princess and Curdie

The Princess and Curdie

by George MacDonald

Science fiction · Fantasy · Children's books

The Princess and Curdie is the sequel to The Princess and the Goblin. The adventure continues with Princess Irene and Curdie a year or two older.

The Story of the Treasure Seekers Being the Adventures of the Bastable Children in Search of a Fortune

The Story of the Treasure Seekers Being the Adventures of the Bastable Children in Search of a Fortune

by E. (Edith) Nesbit

Adventure · Children's books · History

The Story of the Treasure Seekers Being the Adventures of the Bastable Children in Search of a Fortune recounts Dora, Oswald, Dicky, Alice, Noel, and Horace Octavius (H. ) Bastable, and their attempts to assist their widowed father and recover the fortunes of their family.

Five Children and It

Five Children and It

by E. (Edith) Nesbit

Science fiction · Fantasy · Children's books

Five Children and It was originally published the Strand Magazine under the general title The Psammead, or the Gifts, with a segment appearing each month. The stories were then expanded into a novel which was published the same year.

The Wouldbegoods: Being the Further Adventures of the Treasure Seekers

The Wouldbegoods: Being the Further Adventures of the Treasure Seekers

by E. (Edith) Nesbit

Adventure · Children's books · Novel

The Wouldbegoods: Being the Further Adventures of the Treasure Seekers recounts Dora, Oswald, Dicky, Alice, Noel, and Horace Octavius (H. ) Bastable, as well as Daisy and Denis Foulkes, and their attempts to perform good deeds, which usually go awry.

The Phoenix and the Carpet

The Phoenix and the Carpet

by E. (Edith) Nesbit

Science fiction · Fantasy · Children's books

The Phoenix and the Carpet is the second in a trilogy of novels that begins with Five Children and It, and follows the adventures of the same five children: Cyril, Anthea, Robert, Jane and the Lamb. Their mother buys the children a new carpet to replace one from the nursery that they destroyed in an accidental fire.

A House of Pomegranates

A House of Pomegranates

by Oscar Wilde

Short stories · Fantasy · Children's books

A House of Pomegranates is Wilde's second fairy tale collection, following The Happy Prince and Other Tales. He said of the book that it was "intended neither for the British child nor the British public".

The Happy Prince, and Other Tales

The Happy Prince, and Other Tales

by Oscar Wilde

Short stories · Fantasy · Children's books

The Happy Prince, and Other Tales contains five stories that are highly popular among children and frequently read in schools: "The Happy Prince," "The Nightingale and the Rose," "The Selfish Giant," "The Devoted Friend," and "The Remarkable Rocket. " The short stories are valued for their morals, and have been made into animated films.

Alice's Adventures in Wonderland HTML Edition

Alice's Adventures in Wonderland HTML Edition

by Lewis Carroll

Fantasy · Children's books · Novel

Alice's Adventures in Wonderland HTML Edition recounts a little girl named Alice who falls through a rabbit hole into a fantasy world of anthropomorphic creatures. Alice's Adventures in Wonderland HTML Edition is seen as an example of the literary nonsense genre.

Tik-Tok of Oz

Tik-Tok of Oz

by L. Frank (Lyman Frank) Baum

1914 · Science fiction · Fantasy · Children's books

Tik-Tok of Oz has little to do with Tik-Tok and is primarily the quest of the Shaggy Man (introduced in The Road to Oz) to rescue his brother, and his resulting conflict with the Nome King. Tik-Tok of Oz was based on Baum's play The Tik-Tok Man of Oz, which was produced in Los Angeles in spring.

Little Dorrit

Little Dorrit

by Charles Dickens

Romance · Children's books · Novel

Little Dorrit features Amy Dorrit, youngest child of her family, born and raised in the Marshalsea prison for debtors in London. Arthur Clennam encounters her after returning home from a 20-year absence, ready to begin his life anew.

Tanglewood Tales

Tanglewood Tales

by Nathaniel Hawthorne

Fantasy · Children's books

Tanglewood Tales is a re-writing of well-known Greek myths in a volume for children.

Bleak House

Bleak House

by Charles Dickens

Children's books · Novel

Bleak House has many characters and several subplots, and is told partly by the novel's heroine, Esther Summerson, and partly by an omniscient narrator. At the centre of Bleak House is a long-running legal case in the Court of Chancery, Jarndyce and Jarndyce, which comes about because a testator has written several conflicting wills.

by Charles Dickens

Short stories · Children's books

Originally published, Doctor Marigold was extremely successful, as were Dickens's public performances of a play based on the story--fascinating and easy to read. Doctor (it is his given name) Marigold is a "Cheap Jack" or what we would call a street peddler.

The Prince and the Pauper

The Prince and the Pauper

by Mark Twain

Children's books · History · Novel

The Prince and the Pauper was first published Canada, before its publication in the United States. The Prince and the Pauper represents Twain's first attempt at historical fiction.

The Governess; Or, The Little Female Academy

The Governess; Or, The Little Female Academy

by Sarah Fielding

1749 · Children's books · Novel

The Governess; or, The Little Female Academy is a novel published in 1749 by Sarah Fielding. The Governess; Or, The Little Female Academy is the first full-length novel written for children, and is a significant work of 18th-century children's literature.

Rose in Bloom A Sequel to "Eight Cousins"

Rose in Bloom A Sequel to "Eight Cousins"

by Louisa May Alcott

Children's books · Novel

Rose in Bloom A Sequel to "Eight Cousins" depicts the story of a nineteenth-century girl, Rose Campbell, finding her way in society, seeking a profession in philanthropy, and finding a marriage partner. Considered enjoyable by some readers and dull by others, the novel received generally positive reviews.

Jo's Boys

Jo's Boys

by Louisa May Alcott

Children's books · Novel

Jo's Boys is the final book in the unofficial Little Women series. In it, the March sisters' children and the original students of Plumfield, now grown, are caught up in real world troubles as they work towards careers and pursue love.

Wives and Daughters

Wives and Daughters

by Elizabeth Cleghorn Gaskell

Romance · Children's books · Novel

Wives and Daughters was partly written whilst Gaskell was staying with the salon hostess Mary Elizabeth Mohl at her home on the Rue du Bac in Paris. When Mrs Gaskell died suddenly in 1865, it was not quite complete, and the last section was written by Frederick Greenwood.

North and South

North and South

by Elizabeth Cleghorn Gaskell

Children's books · Novel

At first, Gaskell wanted the novel to be titled after the heroine, Margaret Hale, but Charles Dickens, the editor of Household Words, the magazine in which the novel was serialised, insisted on North and South. Gaskell's first novel, Mary Barton, focused on relations between employers and workers in Manchester from the perspective of the working poor; North and South uses a protagonist from southern England to show and comment on the perspectives of mill owners and workers in an industrialising city.

Five Little Peppers Midway

Pollyanna Grows Up is the first of many sequels to Porter's best-selling Pollyanna, but is the only one written by Porter herself; the numerous later additions to the Pollyanna franchise were the work of other authors.

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, Chapters 01 to 05

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, Chapters 01 to 05

by Mark Twain

Adventure · Children's books · Novel

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, Chapters 01 to 05 is commonly named among the Great American Novels, and it is among the first in major American literature to be written throughout in vernacular English, characterized by local color regionalism. Being the direct sequel to The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, it is told in the first person by Huckleberry "Huck" Finn, the narrator of two other later Twain novels (Tom Sawyer Abroad and Tom Sawyer, Detective) and a friend of Tom Sawyer.

What Maisie Knew recounts Maisie Farange, the sensitive daughter of divorced, irresponsible, narcissistic, vindictive, and vile parents, Beale and Ida Farange. What Maisie Knew stays with the title character from earliest childhood to precocious maturity.

A Dog of Flanders

by Ouida

Children's books · Novel

A Dog of Flanders concerns a Flemish boy named Nello and his dog, Patrasche, and is set in Antwerp. In Japan, Korea, Russia, Ukraine and the Philippines, the novel has been a popular children's classic for decades and has been adapted into several Japanese films and anime.
