Where to Read / Gothic

Subject

Gothic

Dread, old houses, and the uncanny, in the literary sense. Covers, when Open Library has them, are loaded from their cover service. Nothing gothic is stored on this host.

Jane Eyre

Jane Eyre

by Charlotte Brontë

An orphan becomes a governess and refuses every arrangement that would make her less than a person. The house at Thornfield holds a secret the marriage plot cannot step around.

Wuthering Heights

Wuthering Heights

by Emily Brontë

Heathcliff and Catherine’s attachment wrecks two households across two generations. The story is told by a tenant and a housekeeper, never by the pair at its center.

Frankenstein

Frankenstein

by Mary Shelley

Victor Frankenstein builds a living being and then abandons it. The creature’s education, and Victor’s refusal to answer for what he made, are the book’s real experiment.

Dracula

Dracula

by Bram Stoker

Jonathan Harker’s travel diary opens onto a count who intends to move to London. The rest of the novel is a coalition of notes, blood, and timetables trying to stop him.

The Picture of Dorian Gray

The Picture of Dorian Gray

by Oscar Wilde

A young man wishes that a portrait would age in his place. Wilde’s only novel follows the wish into influence, cruelty, and a picture locked in a room.

The Hound of the Baskervilles

The Hound of the Baskervilles

by Arthur Conan Doyle

A family legend about a hound on the Devon moor brings Holmes out of a silence Doyle had meant to be permanent. The rational explanation has to earn its place against the fog.

The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

by Robert Louis Stevenson

A London lawyer tries to understand the tie between a respectable doctor and a violent man who shares his house. Stevenson’s short novel is a study of a self split on purpose.

The Yellow Wallpaper

The Yellow Wallpaper

by Charlotte Perkins Gilman

A woman confined to a room for her health begins to watch the pattern in the wallpaper. Gilman’s story is brief and does not offer the cure the physician promised.

The Scarlet Letter

The Scarlet Letter

by Nathaniel Hawthorne

Hester Prynne wears a letter the town requires, while the father of her child stays unnamed. Hawthorne’s novel is about punishment as a public art.
