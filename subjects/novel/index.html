Longer fiction still in the public domain: social worlds, moral arguments, and plots that have outlasted their first printers. Each card below is a catalog entry, not a file stored here.

Pride and Prejudice

Pride and Prejudice

by Jane Austen

1813 · Novel · Romance

Elizabeth Bennet and Mr. Darcy misjudge each other in a comedy of marriage, income, and pride. Austen’s second published novel is still the surest entrance to her shelf.

Emma

Emma

by Jane Austen

1815 · Novel · Romance

Emma Woodhouse has money, leisure, and a talent for arranging other people’s lives. The novel lets her be charming and wrong in the same sentence.

Sense and Sensibility

Sense and Sensibility

by Jane Austen

1811 · Novel · Romance

Two sisters, turned out of their home, try sense and feeling as ways of surviving love and reduced means. It was Austen’s first published novel.

Persuasion

Persuasion

by Jane Austen

1817 · Novel · Romance

Anne Elliot broke an engagement years ago and has had to live quietly with the decision. A return of the man she refused gives the book its second chance.

Jane Eyre

Jane Eyre

by Charlotte Brontë

1847 · Novel · Gothic · Romance

An orphan becomes a governess and refuses every arrangement that would make her less than a person. The house at Thornfield holds a secret the marriage plot cannot step around.

Wuthering Heights

Wuthering Heights

by Emily Brontë

1847 · Novel · Gothic

Heathcliff and Catherine’s attachment wrecks two households across two generations. The story is told by a tenant and a housekeeper, never by the pair at its center.

Frankenstein

Frankenstein

by Mary Shelley

1818 · Novel · Gothic · Science fiction

Victor Frankenstein builds a living being and then abandons it. The creature’s education, and Victor’s refusal to answer for what he made, are the book’s real experiment.

Dracula

Dracula

by Bram Stoker

1897 · Novel · Gothic

Jonathan Harker’s travel diary opens onto a count who intends to move to London. The rest of the novel is a coalition of notes, blood, and timetables trying to stop him.

The Picture of Dorian Gray

The Picture of Dorian Gray

by Oscar Wilde

1890 · Novel · Gothic

A young man wishes that a portrait would age in his place. Wilde’s only novel follows the wish into influence, cruelty, and a picture locked in a room.

The Hound of the Baskervilles

The Hound of the Baskervilles

by Arthur Conan Doyle

1902 · Novel · Mystery · Gothic

A family legend about a hound on the Devon moor brings Holmes out of a silence Doyle had meant to be permanent. The rational explanation has to earn its place against the fog.

Treasure Island

Treasure Island

by Robert Louis Stevenson

1883 · Adventure · Novel

Jim Hawkins finds a map and goes to sea with a crew that is not what the advertisement promised. Long John Silver remains the model of a charming villain.

The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

by Robert Louis Stevenson

1886 · Gothic · Novel

A London lawyer tries to understand the tie between a respectable doctor and a violent man who shares his house. Stevenson’s short novel is a study of a self split on purpose.

A Tale of Two Cities

A Tale of Two Cities

by Charles Dickens

1859 · Novel

London and Paris before and during the French Revolution, joined by a look-alike, a prisoner, and a sacrifice. The opening sentence is famous; the plot earns it.

Great Expectations

Great Expectations

by Charles Dickens

1861 · Novel

Pip is lifted out of a forge by money whose source he misunderstands. Dickens’s late novel is about class, gratitude, and the patron Pip invents in his head.

Moby-Dick

Moby-Dick

by Herman Melville

1851 · Novel · Adventure

Ishmael ships on the Pequod under a captain hunting one white whale. The book stops for whales, work, and metaphysics, then returns to the chase.

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn

by Mark Twain

1884 · Novel · Adventure

Huck Finn fakes his death and rafts the Mississippi with Jim, who is escaping enslavement. Twain’s novel is funny, and it does not let the joke forgive the country it travels.

Little Women

Little Women

by Louisa May Alcott

1868 · Novel

Meg, Jo, Beth, and Amy March grow up in a household short of money and long on opinions. Jo’s wish to write is as much the plot as any marriage.

Anne of Green Gables

Anne of Green Gables

by L. M. Montgomery

1908 · Novel · Children's books

Matthew and Marilla Cuthbert ask for a boy from the orphanage and receive Anne Shirley. She talks, imagines, and slowly makes herself necessary to Avonlea.

The Secret Garden

The Secret Garden

by Frances Hodgson Burnett

1911 · Children's books · Novel

Mary Lennox arrives at a Yorkshire manor and finds a garden no one is allowed to enter. Tending it changes her, and the cousin shut in another room.

The Call of the Wild

The Call of the Wild

by Jack London

1903 · Adventure · Novel

Buck, a stolen California dog, is worked north into the Yukon. London tells the story from the animal’s side of the harness.

The Time Machine

The Time Machine

by H. G. Wells

1895 · Science fiction · Novel

A Victorian inventor rides his machine to a future in which humanity has split. Wells’s novella is a social argument dressed as a traveler’s report.

The War of the Worlds

The War of the Worlds

by H. G. Wells

1898 · Science fiction · Novel

Martian cylinders fall on the English countryside and the narrator walks through the collapse of ordinary life. Wells aimed the invasion at the empire that had invaded others.

The Awakening

The Awakening

by Kate Chopin

1899 · Novel

Edna Pontellier, a wife in Creole New Orleans, stops agreeing that her life is already decided. Chopin’s novel was received coldly and later read as a classic.

Crime and Punishment

Crime and Punishment

by Fyodor Dostoevsky

1866 · Novel

Raskolnikov tests a theory that some people may kill for a higher purpose, and the theory does not survive the act. This entry points to a public-domain English translation.

Anna Karenina

Anna Karenina

by Leo Tolstoy

1877 · Novel · Romance

Anna’s affair and Levin’s search for a workable life run side by side in imperial Russia. Tolstoy gives the social world as much weight as the passion. The link is to a public-domain English translation.

The Count of Monte Cristo

The Count of Monte Cristo

by Alexandre Dumas

1844 · Novel · Adventure

Edmond Dantès is imprisoned on a false charge and comes back, years later, with a new name and a plan. Dumas makes patience itself into an adventure.

Les Misérables

Les Misérables

by Victor Hugo

1862 · Novel

Jean Valjean leaves prison and is pursued across France by Inspector Javert, while Hugo pauses to write about sewers, convents, and justice. The catalog links a public-domain English translation.

Don Quixote

Don Quixote

by Miguel de Cervantes

1605 · Novel · Adventure

A country gentleman renames himself a knight and rides out to enforce the plots of chivalric books. Cervantes’s novel is the comedy of taking reading literally. The link is to a public-domain English translation.

The Scarlet Letter

The Scarlet Letter

by Nathaniel Hawthorne

1850 · Novel · Gothic

Hester Prynne wears a letter the town requires, while the father of her child stays unnamed. Hawthorne’s novel is about punishment as a public art.

The Great Gatsby

The Great Gatsby

by F. Scott Fitzgerald

1925 · Novel

Nick Carraway rents a house next to Jay Gatsby’s parties and slowly learns what the parties are for.

Through the Looking-Glass

Through the Looking-Glass

by Lewis Carroll

Fantasy · Children's books · Novel

Through the Looking-Glass is the sequel to his Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, in which many of the characters were anthropomorphic playing cards. In this second novel the theme is chess.

Moby-Dick; or, The Whale

Moby-Dick; or, The Whale

by Herman Melville

Adventure · Novel

Moby-Dick; or, The Whale turns on the sailor Ishmael's narrative of the maniacal quest of Ahab, captain of the whaling ship Pequod, for vengeance against Moby Dick, the giant white sperm whale that bit off his leg on the ship's previous voyage.

The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete

The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete

by Mark Twain

Adventure · Children's books · Novel

The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Complete is set in the 1830s–1840s in the fictional town of St. Petersburg, which is based on Hannibal, Missouri, where Twain lived as a boy. In the novel, Sawyer has several adventures, often with his friend Huckleberry Finn.

The House of the Seven Gables

The House of the Seven Gables

by Nathaniel Hawthorne

History · Novel

The House of the Seven Gables stays with a New England family and their ancestral home. In the book, Hawthorne explores themes of guilt, retribution, and atonement, and colors the tale with suggestions of the supernatural and witchcraft.

Tom Sawyer Abroad

Tom Sawyer Abroad

by Mark Twain

Adventure · Humor · Novel

Tom Sawyer Abroad features Tom Sawyer and Huckleberry Finn in a parody of adventure stories like those of Jules Verne.

Tom Sawyer, Detective

Tom Sawyer, Detective

by Mark Twain

Mystery · Novel

Tom Sawyer, Detective is a sequel to The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, and Tom Sawyer Abroad. Tom Sawyer attempts to solve a mysterious murder in this burlesque of the immensely popular detective novels of the time.

Far from the Madding Crowd

Far from the Madding Crowd

by Thomas Hardy

Romance · Novel

Far from the Madding Crowd originally appeared anonymously as a monthly serial in Cornhill Magazine, where it gained a wide readership. Far from the Madding Crowd is set in Thomas Hardy's Wessex in rural southwest England, as had been his earlier Under the Greenwood Tree.

Tess of the d'Urbervilles: A Pure Woman

Tess of the d'Urbervilles: A Pure Woman

by Thomas Hardy

Children's books · Novel

Tess of the d'Urbervilles: A Pure Woman initially appeared in a censored and serialised version, published by the British illustrated newspaper The Graphic, then in book form in three volumes, and as a single volume. Although now considered a major novel of the 19th century, Tess of the d'Urbervilles received mixed reviews when it first appeared, in part because it challenged the morals of late Victorian England.

Freckles

Freckles

by Gene Stratton-Porter

Novel

Freckles is primarily set in the Limberlost Swamp area of Indiana, with brief scenes set in Chicago. The title character also appears briefly in Porter's A Girl of the Limberlost.

A Tramp Abroad

A Tramp Abroad

by Mark Twain

Humor · Travel · Novel

A Tramp Abroad details a journey by the author, with his friend Harris (a character created for the book, and based on his closest friend, Joseph Twichell), through central and southern Europe. While the stated goal of the journey is to walk most of the way, the men find themselves using other forms of transport as they traverse the continent.

The Return of the Native

The Return of the Native

by Thomas Hardy

Romance · Novel

The Return of the Native first appeared in the magazine Belgravia, a publication known for its sensationalism, and was presented in 12 monthly instalments from 9 January to 19 December. Because of the novel's controversial themes, Hardy had some difficulty finding a publisher; reviews, however, though somewhat mixed, were generally positive.

The Rise of Silas Lapham

The Rise of Silas Lapham

by William Dean Howells

Novel

The Rise of Silas Lapham stays with the materialistic rise of Silas Lapham from rags to riches, and his ensuing moral susceptibility. Silas earns a fortune in the paint business, but he lacks social standards, which he tries to attain through his daughter's marriage into the aristocratic Corey family.

The Moonstone

The Moonstone

by Wilkie Collins

1868 · Mystery · Novel

The Moonstone is an early example of the modern detective novel, and established many of the ground rules of the modern genre. Its publication was started on 4 January 1868 and was completed on 8 August 1868.

Daddy-Long-Legs

Daddy-Long-Legs

by Jean Webster

Romance · Letters · Novel

Daddy-Long-Legs stays with the protagonist, Jerusha "Judy" Abbott, as she leaves an orphanage and is sent to college by a benefactor whom she has never seen. Daddy-Long-Legs was initially serialized in the April–September issues of the Ladies' Home Journal, and first published in book form by The Century Company.

McTeague: A Story of San Francisco

McTeague: A Story of San Francisco

by Frank Norris

Novel

McTeague: A Story of San Francisco recounts a couple's courtship and marriage, and their subsequent descent into poverty and violence as the result of jealousy and greed. McTeague: A Story of San Francisco was the basis for the films McTeague and Erich von Stroheim's Greed.

Charlotte Temple

Charlotte Temple

by Mrs. Rowson

1794 · Children's books · Novel

Charlotte Temple recounts a schoolgirl, Charlotte Temple, who is seduced by a British officer and brought to America, where she is abandoned, pregnant, sick and in poverty. The first American edition was published in 1794 and the novel became a bestseller.

The Phantom of the Opera

The Phantom of the Opera

by Gaston Leroux

Mystery · Gothic · Novel

The Phantom of the Opera was first published as a serial in Le Gaulois from 23 September to 8 January, and was released in volume form in late March by Pierre Lafitte. The Phantom of the Opera is partly inspired by historical events at the Paris Opera during the nineteenth century, and by an apocryphal tale concerning the use of a former ballet pupil's skeleton in a production of Carl Maria von Weber's Der Freischütz.

The American

The American

by Henry James

Romance · Novel

The American is an uneasy combination of social comedy and melodrama concerning the adventures and misadventures of Christopher Newman, an essentially good-hearted but rather gauche American businessman on his first tour of Europe.

The Europeans

The Europeans

by Henry James

Novel

The Europeans is a comedy contrasting the behaviour and attitudes of two visitors from Europe with those of their relatives living in the "New" World of New England. The Europeans first appeared as a serial in The Atlantic Monthly for July–October.

The Aspern Papers

The Aspern Papers

by Henry James

Novel

One of James's best-known and most acclaimed longer tales, The Aspern Papers is based on the letters Percy Bysshe Shelley wrote to Mary Shelley's stepsister, Claire Clairmont, who saved them until she died. Set in Venice, The Aspern Papers demonstrates James's ability to generate suspense while never neglecting the development of his characters.

A pair of blue eyes

by Thomas Hardy

Romance · Novel

A pair of blue eyes was the first of Hardy's novels to bear his name and was included by Hardy in his "romances and fantasies".

The Octopus : A Story of California

by Frank Norris

The Octopus: A Story of California describes the wheat industry in California, and the conflicts between wheat growers and a railway company. Norris was inspired to write the novel by the Mussel Slough Tragedy involving the Southern Pacific Railroad.

by Anna Sewell

Black Beauty was written from a horse as main character's perspective. She wrote it in the last years of her life, during which she was bedridden and seriously ill.

by Daniel Defoe

Adventure · Children's books · Novel

The Fortunes and Misfortunes of the Famous Moll Flanders purports to be an autobiographical account of the life of the eponymous heroine, detailing her exploits from birth until old age as she uses her wits, cunning and charm to provide herself with financial security. While the novel is now generally attributed to Defoe, and his name is given as the author in modern printings, the original text does not list an author and was presented as an autobiography.

Novel

The Ambassadors is a dark comedy which follows the trip of protagonist Lewis Lambert Strether to Paris, France to bring Chad Newsome, the son of his widowed fiancée, Mrs Newsome, back to the family business. The Ambassadors is written in the third-person, from Strether's point of view.

Just David

Just David

by Eleanor H. (Eleanor Hodgman) Porter

Children's books · Novel

Just David was among the top six bestsellers in cities across the United States, and in July it was the second bestselling novel. Originally published by Houghton Mifflin, it recounts a young boy, David, who must learn to adapt to living with others after the death of his reclusive father; along the way, the villagers and his adoptive parents adapt as much or more to him.

Novel

The Princess of Cleves

by Madame de (Marie-Madeleine Pioche de La Vergne) La Fayette

The Princess of Cleves was regarded by many as the beginning of the modern tradition of the psychological novel and a classic work. Its author is generally held to be Madame de La Fayette.

Manon Lescaut

Manon Lescaut tells a tragic love story about a nobleman (known only as the Chevalier des Grieux) and a common woman (Manon Lescaut). Little Lord Fauntleroy was published as a serial in St. Nicholas Magazine, then as a book by Scribner's (the publisher of St. Nicholas) in New York. Little Lord Fauntleroy was also published that year in November by Frederick Warne & Co.

Heart of Darkness

Heart of Darkness

by Joseph Conrad

Novel

Heart of Darkness is widely regarded as a critique of European colonialism in Africa, whilst also examining the themes of power dynamics and morality. Although Conrad does not name the river on which most of the narrative takes place, at the time of writing, the Congo Free State—the location of the large and economically important Congo River—was a private colony of Belgium's King Leopold II.

Silas Marner

by George Eliot

Children's books · Novel

Silas Marner: The Weaver of Raveloe is the third novel by English author George Eliot; it was published on 2 April. A seemingly simple story of a linen weaver, Silas Marner is notable for its strong realist bent, as well as its sophisticated treatment of a variety of issues ranging from religion to industrialisation to community.
