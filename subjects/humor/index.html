Humor

Satire, comic novels, and books that were meant to be read aloud and laughed at. The jokes are old enough to be public domain; the links are current.

The Hunting of the Snark: An Agony in Eight Fits

The Hunting of the Snark: An Agony in Eight Fits

by Lewis Carroll

Poetry · Children's books · Humor

The Hunting of the Snark: An Agony in Eight Fits is typically categorised as a nonsense poem. It borrows the setting, some creatures, and eight portmanteau words from Carroll's earlier poem "Jabberwocky" in his children's novel Through the Looking-Glass.

A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court

A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court

by Mark Twain

Science fiction · Fantasy · Humor

A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court was originally titled A Yankee in King Arthur's Court. Some early editions are titled A Yankee at the Court of King Arthur.

Tom Sawyer Abroad

Tom Sawyer Abroad

by Mark Twain

Adventure · Humor · Novel

Tom Sawyer Abroad features Tom Sawyer and Huckleberry Finn in a parody of adventure stories like those of Jules Verne.

A Tramp Abroad

A Tramp Abroad

by Mark Twain

Humor · Travel · Novel

A Tramp Abroad details a journey by the author, with his friend Harris (a character created for the book, and based on his closest friend, Joseph Twichell), through central and southern Europe. While the stated goal of the journey is to walk most of the way, the men find themselves using other forms of transport as they traverse the continent.

Northanger Abbey

Northanger Abbey

by Jane Austen

Gothic · Romance · Humor

Although the title page is and the novel was published posthumously in 1817 with Persuasion, Northanger Abbey was completed. From a fondness of Gothic novels and an active imagination distorting her worldview, the story follows Catherine Morland, the naïve young protagonist, as she develops a better understanding of herself and the world around her.

The Pickwick Papers

The Pickwick Papers

by Charles Dickens

Humor · Novel

The Posthumous Papers of the Pickwick Club (also known as The Pickwick Papers) was the first novel by English author Charles Dickens, first published in serial form. Because of his success with Sketches by Boz published, Dickens was asked by the publisher Chapman & Hall to supply descriptions to explain a series of comic "cockney sporting plates" by illustrator Robert Seymour, and to connect them into a novel.

Vanity Fair

Vanity Fair

by William Makepeace Thackeray

History · Humor · Novel

Vanity Fair was published as a single volume in with the subtitle A Novel without a Hero. Vanity Fair is sometimes considered the "principal founder" of the Victorian domestic novel.

The Warden

The Warden

by Anthony Trollope

Humor · Novel

The Warden is the first book in the Chronicles of Barsetshire series, followed by Barchester Towers.

The Idle Thoughts of an Idle Fellow

The Idle Thoughts of an Idle Fellow

by Jerome K. (Jerome Klapka) Jerome

Humor · Letters

The Idle Thoughts of an Idle Fellow was the author’s second published book and it helped establish him as a leading English humorist. While widely considered one of Jerome’s better works, and in spite of using the same style as Three Men in a Boat, it was never as popular as the latter.

Our Mutual Friend

Our Mutual Friend

by Charles Dickens

Romance · Humor · Novel

Our Mutual Friend is the fourteenth and final novel completed by English author Charles Dickens and is one of his most sophisticated works, combining savage satire with social analysis.

Martin Chuzzlewit

Martin Chuzzlewit

by Charles Dickens

Adventure · Humor · Travel

Martin Chuzzlewit was originally serialised between January and July. While he was writing it, Dickens told a friend that he thought it was his best work thus far, but it was one of his least popular novels, judged by sales of the monthly instalments.

Extract from Captain Stormfield's Visit to Heaven

Extract from Captain Stormfield's Visit to Heaven

by Mark Twain

Science fiction · Fantasy · Humor

Extract from Captain Stormfield's Visit to Heaven first appeared in print in Harper's Magazine in December and January, and was published in book form with some revisions. This was the last story published by Twain during his life.

Sartor Resartus: The Life and Opinions of Herr Teufelsdröckh

Sartor Resartus: The Life and Opinions of Herr Teufelsdröckh

by Thomas Carlyle

Philosophy · Humor · Novel

Sartor Resartus: The Life and Opinions of Herr Teufelsdröckh purports to be a commentary on the thought and early life of a German philosopher called Diogenes Teufelsdröckh (which translates as 'Zeus-born Devil's-dung'), author of a tome entitled Clothes: Their Origin and Influence.

The life and opinions of Tristram Shandy, gentleman

The life and opinions of Tristram Shandy, gentleman

by Laurence Sterne

Humor · Novel

The life and opinions of Tristram Shandy, gentleman purports to be a memoir, but the titular Tristram is an effusive and digressive narrator who begins the story with his conception and does not reach a description of his birth until the third volume.

A Horse's Tale

A Horse's Tale

by Mark Twain

Adventure · Children's books · Humor

A Horse's Tale is a novel written by American author Mark Twain (Samuel Clemens), written partially in the voice of Soldier Boy, who is Buffalo Bill's favorite horse, at a fictional frontier outpost with the U.

The Comedy of Errors

The Comedy of Errors

by William Shakespeare

Plays · Humor

The Comedy of Errors is one of William Shakespeare's earliest plays. The Comedy of Errors is his shortest and one of his most farcical comedies, with a major part of the humour coming from slapstick and mistaken identity, in addition to puns and word play.

The Merry Wives of Windsor

The Merry Wives of Windsor

by William Shakespeare

Plays · Humor

The Merry Wives of Windsor or Sir John Falstaff and the Merry Wives of Windsor is a comedy by William Shakespeare first published, though believed to have been written or before. The Windsor of the play's title is a reference to the town of Windsor, the location of Windsor Castle, in Berkshire, England.

My Buried Treasure

My Buried Treasure

by Richard Harding Davis

Short stories · Adventure · Humor

My Buried Treasure was published as a book, but is set in the 18th century, and tells a story of "buccaneers and buried gold". My Buried Treasure is considered a coming-of-age story, and is noted for its atmosphere, characters, and action.

Tartarin of Tarascon

Tartarin of Tarascon

by Alphonse Daudet

Humor · Novel

Tartarin of Tarascon (French: Tartarin de Tarascon) is a novel written by the French author Alphonse Daudet.

Extracts from Adam's Diary, translated from the original ms

Extracts from Adam's Diary, translated from the original ms

by Mark Twain

History · Humor · Religion

Extracts from Adam's Diary, translated from the original ms was first published in The Niagara Book, and was collected in Twain's book My Debut as a Literary Person with Other Essays and Stories. "Extracts from Adam's Diary" was first published as a book by Harper & Brothers, with numerous illustrations by Frederick Strothmann.

Penguin Island

Penguin Island

by Anatole France

Adventure · Humor · Novel

Penguin Island (; French: L'Île des Pingouins) is a satirical fictional history by French author Anatole France.

Diary of a Pilgrimage

Diary of a Pilgrimage

by Jerome K. (Jerome Klapka) Jerome

1891 · History · Humor · Travel

Diary of a Pilgrimage tells of Jerome's first visit to Germany, a trip he undertakes with his friend "B" to see the Oberammergau Passion Play.

Three men on the bummel

Three men on the bummel

by Jerome K. (Jerome Klapka) Jerome

1900 · Humor · Novel

Three men on the bummel was published in 1900, eleven years after his most famous work, Three Men in a Boat. The sequel brings back the three companions who figured in Three Men in a Boat, this time on a bicycle tour through Germany.

Is Shakespeare Dead? From My Autobiography

Is Shakespeare Dead? From My Autobiography

by Mark Twain

Humor · Letters

Is Shakespeare Dead? From My Autobiography explores the controversy over the authorship of the Shakespearean literary canon via satire, anecdote, and extensive quotation of contemporary authors on the subject.

A House-Boat on the Styx

A House-Boat on the Styx

by John Kendrick Bangs

1895 · Humor · Novel

The original full title was A House-Boat on the Styx: Being Some Account of the Divers Doings of the Associated Shades. A House-Boat on the Styx was first published by Harper Brothers in with illustrations by Peter Newell (24 plates).

Our American Cousin

Our American Cousin

by Tom Taylor

1919 · Plays · Humor

Our American Cousin is a farce about an awkward, boorish American man named Asa Trenchard who is introduced to his aristocratic English relatives when he goes to England to claim the family estate. The play premiered with great success at Laura Keene's Theatre in New York City, with Laura Keene in the cast, the title character played by Joseph Jefferson, and Edward Askew Sothern playing Lord Dundreary.

Fenimore Cooper's Literary Offences

Fenimore Cooper's Literary Offences

by Mark Twain

History · Humor · Letters

Fenimore Cooper's Literary Offences draws on examples from The Deerslayer and The Pathfinder from Cooper's Leatherstocking Tales. The essay is characteristic of Twain's biting, derisive, and highly satirical style of literary criticism, a form he also used to deride such authors as Oliver Goldsmith, George Eliot, Jane Austen, and Robert Louis Stevenson.

Roughing It

Roughing It

by Mark Twain

Humor · Travel

Roughing It was written and published, following his first travel book The Innocents Abroad.

Christian Science

Christian Science

by Mark Twain

Humor · Religion · Science

Although Twain was interested in mental healing and the ideas behind Christian Science, he was hostile towards its founder, Mary Baker Eddy (–1910).

Barry Lyndon

Barry Lyndon

by William Makepeace Thackeray

History · Humor · Novel

Thackeray, who based the novel on the life and exploits of the Anglo-Irish rake and fortune-hunter Andrew Robinson Stoney, later reissued it under the title The Memoirs of Barry Lyndon, Esq. Barry Lyndon is narrated by Lyndon himself, who functions as a quintessentially unreliable narrator.

The Adventures of Mr. Verdant Green

The Adventures of Mr. Verdant Green

by Cuthbert Bede

1908 · History · Humor · Novel

The Adventures of Mr. Verdant Green covers the exploits of Verdant Green, a first-year student at Oxford University. Green is an undergraduate at the fictional Brazenface College.

Peter Bell the Third

Peter Bell the Third

by Percy Bysshe Shelley

Poetry · Humor

"Peter Bell the Third" is a satire and parody by the English Romantic poet Percy Bysshe Shelley. Peter Bell the Third was not published in The Poetical Works of Percy Bysshe Shelley.

The Merry Devil of Edmonton

The Merry Devil of Edmonton

by William Shakespeare

Plays · Humor

The Merry Devil of Edmonton was at one point attributed to William Shakespeare, but is now considered part of the Shakespeare Apocrypha.

The Way We Live Now

The Way We Live Now

by Anthony Trollope

Humor · Novel

The Way We Live Now is one of the last significant Victorian novels to have been published in monthly parts. The Way We Live Now is one of Trollope's longest, comprising 100 chapters, and is particularly rich in sub-plot.

The Day of the Dog

The Day of the Dog

by George Barr McCutcheon

1904 · Romance · Adventure · Humor

The Day of the Dog is a novel written by George Barr McCutcheon in 1904.

The Blunderer

The Blunderer

by Molière

Plays · Humor

The Blunderer is generally believed to have been first acted at Lyons, whilst Moliere and his troupe were in the provinces. In the month of November it was played for the first time in Paris, where it obtained a great and well-deserved success.

St. Patrick's Day; Or, The Scheming Lieutenant: A Farce in One Act

St. Patrick's Day; Or, The Scheming Lieutenant: A Farce in One Act

by Richard Brinsley Sheridan

Plays · Humor

St. Patrick's Day; Or, The Scheming Lieutenant: A Farce in One Act is said to have completed by the author within two days. Sheridan wrote the two-act farce for the benefit performance of lead actor Laurence Clinch, who had so successfully played Sir Lucius O'Trigger in his previous play The Rivals.

The Consolidator; or, Memoirs of Sundry Transactions from the World in the Moon

The Consolidator; or, Memoirs of Sundry Transactions from the World in the Moon

by Daniel Defoe

Science fiction · Fantasy · Humor

The Consolidator; or, Memoirs of Sundry Transactions from the World in the Moon is a fictional adventure by Daniel Defoe published. The Consolidator; or, Memoirs of Sundry Transactions from the World in the Moon is a satirical novel that mixes fantasy, political satire, and social satire.

The Eustace Diamonds

The Eustace Diamonds

by Anthony Trollope

Humor · Novel

The Eustace Diamonds is considered the third of what are now called the Palliser novels, though Trollope himself didn't see it as part of the series and the characters of Plantagenet Palliser, Lady Glencora and their circle remain mostly in the background.

The Three Jovial Huntsmen

The Three Jovial Huntsmen

by Randolph Caldecott

1880 · Adventure · Children's books · Humor

The Three Jovial Huntsmen (1880) was a popular British picture book illustrated by Randolph Caldecott, engraved and printed by Edmund Evans and published by George Routledge & Sons in London. The toy book, which is a variant of the folklore song The Three Huntsmen (sometimes called the Three Jolly Huntsmen), was well-received, selling tens of thousands of copies.

Candide

Candide

by Voltaire

Humor · Novel

Candide, ou l'Optimisme ( kon-DEED or kahn-DEED, French: [kɑ̃did] ) is a French satire written by Voltaire, a philosopher of the Age of Enlightenment, first published. A young man, Candide, lives a sheltered life in an Edenic paradise, being indoctrinated with Leibnizian optimism by his mentor, Professor Pangloss.

Chapters from My Autobiography

Chapters from My Autobiography

by Mark Twain

Humor

Chapters from My Autobiography are 25 pieces of autobiographical work published by American author Mark Twain in the North American Review between September and December. Rather than following the standard form of an autobiography, they comprise a rambling gathering of anecdotes and ruminations.

The Confidence-Man: His Masquerade

The Confidence-Man: His Masquerade

by Herman Melville

Humor · Novel

The Confidence-Man: His Masquerade, first published in New York on April Fool's Day, is the ninth and final novel by American writer Herman Melville. The Confidence-Man: His Masquerade was published on the exact day of the novel's setting.

The Big Bow Mystery

The Big Bow Mystery

by Israel Zangwill

1895 · Mystery · Humor

The Big Bow Mystery was originally serialised in The Star newspaper, before being published as a novel the following year. Set in London's East End, it is one of the earliest examples of the locked-room mystery genre.

Holiday House: A Series of Tales

Holiday House: A Series of Tales

by Catherine Sinclair

1839 · Children's books · Humor · Novel

Holiday House: A Series of Tales was first published in Edinburgh by William Whyte & Co. Holiday House is set in Edinburgh at some point before.

The Comic History of Rome

by Gilbert Abbott À Beckett

1850 · Fantasy · History · Humor

The Comic History of Rome was published by Bradbury and Evans, with text by the English humorist Gilbert Abbott à Beckett and comic illustrations by John Leech, famous for his illustrations for the novella A Christmas Carol by Charles Dickens. The Old Debauchees. A Comedy

The Old Debauchees. A Comedy

by Henry Fielding

Plays · Humor

The Old Debauchees. A Comedy premiered alongside The Covent-Garden Tragedy on 1 June at the Theatre Royal, Drury Lane, and was later revived as The Debauchees; or, The Jesuit Caught. The play recounts a Catholic priest's unsuccessful attempt to manipulate a man into seducing his own daughter.

The Heroine

The Heroine

by Eaton Stannard Barrett

1909 · Gothic · Humor · Novel

The Heroine is a quixotic satire, in which the protagonist displays the hallmarks of "heroinism" exemplified by Germaine de Staël's novel Corinne ou l'Italie and goes through a series of Gothic adventures inspired by Ann Radcliffe's novels.

A Pickle for the Knowing Ones

A Pickle for the Knowing Ones

by Timothy Dexter

1848 · Philosophy · Humor

A Pickle for the Knowing Ones uses spelling and grammar that are often considered poor, and contains almost no punctuation. Dexter was a rich businessman and an eccentric, known for gaining his wealth through ill-advised but ultimately lucky investments like sending coals to Newcastle at the time of a miners' strike.

Letters of Peregrine Pickle

Letters of Peregrine Pickle

by George P. (George Putnam) Upton

1869 · Humor · Letters · Novel

Letters of Peregrine Pickle recounts an egotistical man who experiences luck and misfortunes in the height of 18th-century European society.

The Westminster Alice

The Westminster Alice

by Saki

1902 · Plays · Humor

The Westminster Alice is a political parody of Lewis Carroll's two books, Alice's Adventures in Wonderland and Through the Looking-Glass. The Westminster Alice features 48 drawings by Francis Carruthers Gould, after the originals by John Tenniel.

The Hole Book

The Hole Book

by Peter Newell

1908 · Poetry · Children's books · Humor

The Hole Book stays with an accidentally-fired bullet as it travels through various scenes and objects in a town. The physical book features a literal, die-cut hole on each page, which is incorporated into the illustrations of havoc wreaked along bullet's path.

Salmagundi

Salmagundi

by William Irving

Humor

Salmagundi; or The Whim-whams and Opinions of Launcelot Langstaff, Esq. & Others, commonly referred to as Salmagundi, was a 19th-century satirical periodical created and written by American writer Washington Irving, his oldest brother William, and James Kirke Paulding.
