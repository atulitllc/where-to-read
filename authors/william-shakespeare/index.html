William Shakespeare

William Shakespeare (1564–1616) is represented here by 167 public-domain titles, including A Lover's Complaint, A Lover's Complaint, and A Midsummer Night's Dream. This page is a shelf list: the books are read on Open Library, not stored here.

Venus and Adonis

Venus and Adonis

by William Shakespeare

Poetry · Fantasy

Venus and Adonis is considered likely to be Shakespeare's first publication. The poem recounts Venus, the goddess of Love; of her unrequited love; and of her attempted seduction of Adonis, an extremely handsome young man, who would rather go hunting.

King Henry VI, Part 1

King Henry VI, Part 1

by William Shakespeare

Plays · History

King Henry VI, Part 1 is set during the lifetime of King Henry VI of England. Henry VI, Part 1 takes up the loss of England's French territories and the political machinations leading up to the Wars of the Roses, as the English political system is torn apart by personal squabbles and petty jealousy.

The Comedy of Errors

The Comedy of Errors

by William Shakespeare

Plays · Humor

The Comedy of Errors is one of William Shakespeare's earliest plays. The Comedy of Errors is his shortest and one of his most farcical comedies, with a major part of the humour coming from slapstick and mistaken identity, in addition to puns and word play.

Titus Andronicus

Titus Andronicus

by William Shakespeare

Plays · History

The Lamentable Tragedy of Titus Andronicus, often shortened to Titus Andronicus, is a tragedy by William Shakespeare, believed to have been written. Titus Andronicus is thought to be Shakespeare's first tragedy and is often seen as his attempt to emulate the violent and bloody revenge plays of his contemporaries, which were extremely popular with audiences throughout the 16th century.

The Two Gentlemen of Verona

The Two Gentlemen of Verona

by William Shakespeare

Plays

The Two Gentlemen of Verona is considered by some to be Shakespeare's first play, and is often seen as showing his first tentative steps in laying out some of the themes and motifs with which he would later deal in more detail; for example, it is the first of his plays in which a heroine dresses as a boy.

Love's Labour's Lost

Love's Labour's Lost

by William Shakespeare

Plays

Love's Labour's Lost is one of William Shakespeare's early comedies, believed to have been written in the mid-1590s for a performance at the Inns of Court before Queen Elizabeth I. Love's Labour's Lost stays with the King of Navarre and his three companions as they attempt to swear off the company of women for three years in order to focus on study and fasting.

Romeo and Juliet

Romeo and Juliet

by William Shakespeare

Plays · Romance

The Tragedy of Romeo and Juliet, often shortened to Romeo and Juliet, is a tragedy written by William Shakespeare about the romance between two young Italians from feuding families. Romeo and Juliet was among Shakespeare's most popular plays during his lifetime and, along with Hamlet, is one of his most frequently performed.

King Henry IV, Part 1

King Henry IV, Part 1

by William Shakespeare

Plays · History

King Henry IV, Part 1 was composed in the later years of the reign of Elizabeth I, when questions of succession and political stability were prominent. Set in England in the early 1400s during the reign of Henry IV, the play depicts rebellion against the crown alongside the development of Prince Hal, the future Henry V, and examines themes of leadership and the formation of the heir apparent.

The Merry Wives of Windsor

The Merry Wives of Windsor

by William Shakespeare

Plays · Humor

The Merry Wives of Windsor or Sir John Falstaff and the Merry Wives of Windsor is a comedy by William Shakespeare first published, though believed to have been written or before. The Windsor of the play's title is a reference to the town of Windsor, the location of Windsor Castle, in Berkshire, England.

King Henry IV, Part 2

King Henry IV, Part 2

by William Shakespeare

Plays · History

King Henry IV, Part 2 is the third part of a tetralogy, preceded by Richard II and Henry IV, Part 1 and succeeded by Henry V. The play is often seen as an extension of aspects of Henry IV, Part 1, rather than a straightforward continuation of the historical narrative, placing more emphasis on the highly popular character of Falstaff and introducing other comic figures as part of his entourage, including Ancient Pistol, Doll Tearsheet, and Justice Robert Shallow.

King Henry V

King Henry V

by William Shakespeare

Plays · History

King Henry V recounts King Henry V of England, focusing on events immediately before and after the Battle of Agincourt during the Hundred Years' War. In the First Quarto text, it was titled The Cronicle History of Henry the fift, and The Life of Henry the Fifth in the First Folio text.

Julius Caesar

Julius Caesar

by William Shakespeare

Plays · History

The Tragedy of Julius Caesar (First Folio title: The Tragedie of Ivlivs Cæsar), often shortened to Julius Caesar, is a historical tragedy by William Shakespeare, believed to have been written and first performed. The play portrays the political conspiracy that led to the assassination of the Roman dictator Julius Caesar and Rome's subsequent civil war.

As You Like It

As You Like It

by William Shakespeare

Plays

As You Like It follows its heroine Rosalind as she flees persecution in her uncle's court, accompanied by her cousin Celia to find safety and, eventually, love, in the Forest of Arden. In the forest, they encounter a variety of memorable characters, notably the melancholy traveller Jaques, who speaks one of Shakespeare's most famous speeches ("All the world's a stage") and provides a sharp contrast to the other characters in the play, always observing and disputing the hardships of life in the country.

Hamlet

Hamlet

by William Shakespeare

Plays

The Tragedy of Hamlet, Prince of Denmark, often shortened to Hamlet, is a tragedy written by William Shakespeare. Set in Denmark, the play depicts Prince Hamlet and his attempts to exact revenge against his uncle, Claudius, who has murdered Hamlet's father in order to seize his throne and marry Hamlet's mother.

Twelfth Night

Twelfth Night

by William Shakespeare

Plays

Twelfth Night, or What You Will is a romantic comedy by William Shakespeare, believed to have been written around – as a Twelfth Night entertainment for the close of the Christmas season. The play turns on the twins Viola and Sebastian, who are separated in a shipwreck.

Troilus and Cressida

Troilus and Cressida

by William Shakespeare

1714 · Plays

At Troy during the Trojan War, Troilus and Cressida begin a love affair. Cressida is forced to leave Troy to join her father in the Greek camp.

Othello

Othello

by William Shakespeare

Plays

The Tragedy of Othello, the Moor of Venice, often shortened to Othello, is a tragedy written by William Shakespeare. Set in Venice and Cyprus, the play depicts the Moorish military commander Othello as he is manipulated by his ensign, Iago, into suspecting his wife Desdemona of infidelity.

King Lear

King Lear

by William Shakespeare

Plays

The Tragedy of King Lear, often shortened to King Lear, is a tragedy written by William Shakespeare in late or early. Set in pre-Roman Britain, the play depicts the consequences of King Lear's love-test, in which he divides his power and land according to the praise of his daughters.

The Tragedy of Macbeth

The Tragedy of Macbeth

by William Shakespeare

Plays

The Tragedy of Macbeth, often shortened to Macbeth, is a tragedy by William Shakespeare, estimated to have been first performed. The Tragedy of Macbeth dramatises the physically violent and damaging psychological effects of political ambitions and power.

Coriolanus

Coriolanus

by William Shakespeare

Plays

Coriolanus ( or ) is a tragedy by William Shakespeare, believed to have been written. The play is based on the life of the legendary Roman leader Gnaeus Marcius Coriolanus.

Cymbeline

Cymbeline

by William Shakespeare

Plays · Romance · History

Although it is listed as a tragedy in the First Folio, modern critics often classify Cymbeline as a romance or even a comedy. Like Othello and The Winter's Tale, it takes up the themes of innocence and jealousy.

King Richard III

King Richard III

by William Shakespeare

Plays · History

King Richard III is labelled a history in the First Folio and is usually considered one. In the quarto edition and elsewhere it is listed as a tragedy given its meditations on conscience, decision making, and death.

by William Shakespeare

Plays

Pericles, Prince of Tyre was published in as a quarto, was not included in Shakespeare's collections of works until the third folio, and the main inspiration for the play was Gower's Confessio Amantis.

The Passionate Pilgrim

The Passionate Pilgrim

by William Shakespeare

Poetry

The Passionate Pilgrim ( or ) is an anthology of 21 poems collected and published by William Jaggard that were attributed to "W. Shakespeare" on the title page, only five of which are considered authentically Shakespearean.

Mucedorus

Mucedorus

by William Shakespeare

Poetry · Plays · Romance

A Most pleasant Comedie of Mucedorus the Kings Sonne of Valentia, and Amadine the Kinges daughter of Aragon, commonly called Mucedorus, is an Elizabethan romantic comedy, first performed and regularly revived until the Restoration.

Shakespeare's First Folio

Shakespeare's First Folio

by William Shakespeare

Plays

Shakespeare's First Folio is considered one of the most influential books ever published. Printed in folio format and containing 36 of Shakespeare's plays, it was prepared by Shakespeare's colleagues John Heminges and Henry Condell.

The Merry Devil of Edmonton

The Merry Devil of Edmonton

by William Shakespeare

Plays · Humor

The Merry Devil of Edmonton was at one point attributed to William Shakespeare, but is now considered part of the Shakespeare Apocrypha.
