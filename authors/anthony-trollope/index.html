Anthony Trollope

Anthony Trollope (1815–1882) is represented here by 28 public-domain titles, including A Ride Across Palestine, An Eye for an Eye, and Barchester Towers. This page is a shelf list: the books are read on Open Library, not stored here.

The Warden

The Warden

Humor · Novel

The Warden is the first book in the Chronicles of Barsetshire series, followed by Barchester Towers.

The Prime Minister

The Prime Minister

Novel

The Prime Minister is the fifth of the "Palliser" series of novels. Robert Caro has called it "the greatest political novel I ever read.

Framley Parsonage

Framley Parsonage

Novel

Framley Parsonage was first published in serial form in the Cornhill Magazine, then in book form in April. Framley Parsonage is the fourth book in the Chronicles of Barsetshire series, preceded by Doctor Thorne and followed by The Small House at Allington.

Doctor Thorne

Doctor Thorne

Romance · History · Novel

Doctor Thorne is the third book in his Chronicles of Barsetshire series, between Barchester Towers and Framley Parsonage. The idea of the plot was suggested to Trollope by his brother Thomas.

Barchester Towers

Barchester Towers

Novel

Barchester Towers is the second book in the Chronicles of Barsetshire series, preceded by The Warden and followed by Doctor Thorne. In his autobiography Trollope wrote "In the writing of Barchester Towers I took great delight.

The Small House at Allington first appeared as a serial in the Cornhill Magazine, running from the July–December issue to the July–December issue of the following year. In it was published as an independent work in two volumes.

The Kellys and the O'Kellys

The Way We Live Now

The Way We Live Now

Humor · Novel

The Way We Live Now is one of the last significant Victorian novels to have been published in monthly parts. The Way We Live Now is one of Trollope's longest, comprising 100 chapters, and is particularly rich in sub-plot.

The Eustace Diamonds

The Eustace Diamonds

Humor · Novel

The Eustace Diamonds is considered the third of what are now called the Palliser novels, though Trollope himself didn't see it as part of the series and the characters of Plantagenet Palliser, Lady Glencora and their circle remain mostly in the background.

Nina Balatka

John Caldigate

John Caldigate

Australia, England, and gold mines and mining, in Anthony Trollope’s John Caldigate.

Mr. Scarborough's Family

Mr. Scarborough's Family

Novel

Mr. Scarborough's Family was his forty-fifth novel and was left incomplete at the time of his death during its serialization. Mr. Scarborough's Family was originally serialized in All the Year Round from 17 May 1882, to 16 June.

The Claverings

The Claverings

Romance · Novel

The Claverings recounts a young man starting out in life, who must find himself a profession and a wife; and of a young woman who makes a marriage of convenience and must accept the consequences of her decision.

An Eye for an Eye

An Eye for an Eye

Novel

In An Eye for an Eye, Publication in the form of a two volume novel was timed to coincide with the issue of the final serialized episodes in January. An Eye for an Eye is one of five novels which Trollope set mainly in Ireland, although much of the story takes place at Scroope Manor in Dorsetshire, the ancestral home of the ageing Earl of Scroope.

Phineas Finn The Irish Member

Phineas Finn The Irish Member

Novel

Phineas Finn The Irish Member is the second of the "Palliser" series of novels. Its sequel, Phineas Redux, is the fourth novel in the series.

Phineas Redux

Phineas Redux

Novel

Phineas Redux is a novel by Anthony Trollope, first published between and as a serial in The Graphic. Phineas Redux is the fourth of the "Palliser" series of novels and is a sequel to the second book of the series, Phineas Finn.

Thackeray

Thackeray

Can You Forgive Her?

Can You Forgive Her?

Romance · Novel

Can You Forgive Her? is the first of six novels in the Palliser series, also known as the Parliamentary Novels. Can You Forgive Her? stays with three parallel stories of courtship and marriage and the decisions of three women: Alice Vavasor, her cousin Glencora Palliser, and her aunt Arabella Greenow.

The Struggles of Brown, Jones, and Robinson By One of the Firm

The Struggles of Brown, Jones, and Robinson By One of the Firm

Humor · Novel

Satire, Humorous stories, daily life in Great Britain, and Advertising, in Anthony Trollope’s Struggles of Brown, Jones, and Robinson By One of the Firm.

Sir Harry Hotspur of Humblethwaite

Sir Harry Hotspur of Humblethwaite

Novel

Sir Harry Hotspur of Humblethwaite offers psychological dissection of the issues of inheritance, filial duty, noblesse oblige, gentlemanly behaviour, repentance and love, all hung upon the story of the wooing and losing of Sir Harry Hotspur's daughter (and heir to his property), Emily, by their "scamp" of a cousin (and heir to Sir Harry's baronetcy), Captain George Hotspur.

South Australia and Western Australia

South Australia and Western Australia

Adventure · History

In South Australia and Western Australia, in Australia, sport is an important part of the country's culture and dates back to the early colonial period. The first of the country's mainstream sports to become established in order of their organisation were cricket, football, rugby union, tennis, soccer, basketball, netball and rugby league.
