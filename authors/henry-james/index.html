Henry James

Henry James (1843–1916) is represented here by 25 public-domain titles, including A London Life, and Other Tales, A Passionate Pilgrim, and A Small Boy and Others. This page is a shelf list: the books are read on Open Library, not stored here.

The American

The American

Romance · Novel

The American is an uneasy combination of social comedy and melodrama concerning the adventures and misadventures of Christopher Newman, an essentially good-hearted but rather gauche American businessman on his first tour of Europe.

The Europeans

The Europeans

Novel

The Europeans is a comedy contrasting the behaviour and attitudes of two visitors from Europe with those of their relatives living in the "New" World of New England. The Europeans first appeared as a serial in The Atlantic Monthly for July–October.

Daisy Miller: A Study

Daisy Miller: A Study

Novel

In Daisy Miller: A Study, Daisy Miller, A Study is a novella by Henry James that first appeared in The Cornhill Magazine in June–July, and in book form the following year. Daisy Miller: A Study portrays the courtship of the beautiful American girl Daisy Miller by Winterbourne, a sophisticated compatriot of hers.

The Turn of the Screw

The Turn of the Screw

Mystery · Gothic · Children's books

In The Turn of the Screw, on October 7,, it was Collected in The Two Magics, published by Macmillan in New York City and Heinemann in London. The novella follows a governess who, caring for two children at a remote country house, becomes convinced that they are haunted.

The Aspern Papers

The Aspern Papers

Novel

One of James's best-known and most acclaimed longer tales, The Aspern Papers is based on the letters Percy Bysshe Shelley wrote to Mary Shelley's stepsister, Claire Clairmont, who saved them until she died. Set in Venice, The Aspern Papers demonstrates James's ability to generate suspense while never neglecting the development of his characters.

The Ambassadors

The Ambassadors

Novel

The Ambassadors is a dark comedy which follows the trip of protagonist Lewis Lambert Strether to Paris, France to bring Chad Newsome, the son of his widowed fiancée, Mrs Newsome, back to the family business. The Ambassadors is written in the third-person, from Strether's point of view.

The Lesson of the Master

The Lesson of the Master

Novel

In The Lesson of the Master, the Guardian writer Jonathan Jones described the story as that of "a writer [who] must choose between the perfection of art and the seductive compromises of real life.

The Patagonia

The Patagonia

Travel · Novel

Passenger ships, ocean travel, ocean liners, and Americans in travel, in Henry James’ Patagonia.

The Real Thing and Other Tales

The Real Thing and Other Tales

1921 · Short stories

The Portrait of a Lady — Volume 1

The Portrait of a Lady — Volume 1

Romance · Novel

The Portrait of a Lady is one of James's most popular novels and is regarded by critics as one of his finest. The Portrait of a Lady recounts a spirited young American woman, Isabel Archer, who, "affronting her destiny," finds it overwhelming.

The Point of View

The Point of View

Travel · Novel

Daily life in United States, Europeans in United States, and travel in United States, in Henry James’ Point of View.

The Author of Beltraffio

The Author of Beltraffio

1911 · Novel

In The Author of Beltraffio, this macabre account of desperate family infighting eventually leads to a tragic conclusion. Although the father in the story is a novelist, the tale concentrates far more on his family relationships than on his special concerns as a writer, though some of those concerns affect the outcome.

The Golden Bowl — Complete

The Golden Bowl — Complete

Novel

In The Golden Bowl, Set in England, this complex, intense study of marriage and adultery completes what some critics have called the "major phase" of James's career. The Golden Bowl explores the inextricably woven and intertwined web of interrelationships between a father, Adam Verver, his daughter, Maggie Verver, and their respective spouses, Adam's wife Charlotte Stant, and Maggie's husband, Prince Amerigo.

Italian Hours

Italian Hours

Travel · Letters

Italian Hours collected essays that James had written over nearly forty years about a country he knew and loved well. James extensively revised and sometimes expanded the essays to create a more consistent whole.

What Maisie Knew

What Maisie Knew

Children's books · Novel

What Maisie Knew recounts Maisie Farange, the sensitive daughter of divorced, irresponsible, narcissistic, vindictive, and vile parents, Beale and Ida Farange. What Maisie Knew stays with the title character from earliest childhood to precocious maturity.

The Awkward Age

The Awkward Age

Novel

In The Awkward Age, Originally conceived as a brief, light story about the complications created in her family's social set by a young girl coming of age, the novel expanded into a general treatment of decadence and corruption in English fin de siècle life.

A Passionate Pilgrim

A Passionate Pilgrim

Novel

A Passionate Pilgrim was the earliest fiction that James included in the New York Edition of his works. Set in England, the tale shows James' strong interest in the contrast between the Old World and the New.

A London Life, and Other Tales

A London Life, and Other Tales

Short stories

In A London Life, and Other Tales, a London Life is a novella by Henry James, first published in Scribner's Magazine. The plot revolves around a crumbling marriage and its impact on many other people, especially Laura Wing, the sister of the soon-to-be-divorced wife.

A Small Boy and Others

A Small Boy and Others

Literature

A Small Boy and Others covers James' earliest years and discusses his intellectually active family, his intermittent schooling, and his first trips to Europe.

The Wings of the Dove, Volume II

The Wings of the Dove, Volume II

Romance · Novel

The Wings of the Dove recounts Milly Theale, an American heiress stricken with a serious disease, and her effect on the people around her. Some of these people befriend Milly with honourable motives, while others are more self-interested.

The Two Magics: The Turn of the Screw, Covering End

The Two Magics: The Turn of the Screw, Covering End

1898 · Mystery · Children's books · Novel

In The Two Magics: The Turn of the Screw, Covering End, the Turn of the Screw is an 1898 gothic horror novella by Henry James which first appeared in serial format in Collier's Weekly from January 27 to April 16, 1898. On October 7, 1898, it was Collected in The Two Magics, published by Macmillan in New York City and Heinemann in London.

The Birthplace

The Birthplace

Novel

In The Birthplace, a witty satire on the excesses of bardolatry, the story reflects James's skepticism about the authorship of Shakespeare's plays. Beyond the narrow scholarly issue, the story also shows a typically imaginative Jamesian protagonist inventing an alternative reality in his lecture on the Bard's supposed childhood activities.

English Hours

English Hours

Travel · Letters

English Hours collected various essays James had written on England over a period of more than thirty years, beginning in the 1870s. The essays had originally appeared in such periodicals as The Nation, The Century Magazine, Scribner's Magazine, The Galaxy and Lippincott's Magazine.
