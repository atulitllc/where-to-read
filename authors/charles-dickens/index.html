Author

Charles Dickens

Dickens filled novels with institutions, comic speech, and people the city would rather not count. The books here run from a short Christmas tale to the long arc of Great Expectations.

A Christmas Carol

A Christmas Carol

by Charles Dickens

1843 · Short stories

Ebenezer Scrooge is walked through his past, the present he refuses to see, and a future he can still change. Dickens wrote it quickly, and it became the Christmas story people mean.

A Tale of Two Cities

A Tale of Two Cities

by Charles Dickens

1859 · Novel

London and Paris before and during the French Revolution, joined by a look-alike, a prisoner, and a sacrifice. The opening sentence is famous; the plot earns it.

Great Expectations

Great Expectations

by Charles Dickens

1861 · Novel

Pip is lifted out of a forge by money whose source he misunderstands. Dickens’s late novel is about class, gratitude, and the patron Pip invents in his head.

The Mystery of Edwin Drood

The Mystery of Edwin Drood

by Charles Dickens

Mystery · Novel

The Mystery of Edwin Drood was his final novel and was left unfinished when he died. Though the novel is named after the character Edwin Drood, it focuses more on Drood's uncle, John Jasper, a precentor, choirmaster and opium addict, who lusts after his pupil, Rosa Bud.

The Pickwick Papers

The Pickwick Papers

by Charles Dickens

Humor · Novel

The Posthumous Papers of the Pickwick Club (also known as The Pickwick Papers) was the first novel by English author Charles Dickens, first published in serial form. Because of his success with Sketches by Boz published, Dickens was asked by the publisher Chapman & Hall to supply descriptions to explain a series of comic "cockney sporting plates" by illustrator Robert Seymour, and to connect them into a novel.

Master Humphrey's Clock

Master Humphrey's Clock

by Charles Dickens

History · Novel

Master Humphrey's Clock began with a frame story in which Master Humphrey tells about himself and his small circle of friends (which includes Mr. Pickwick), and their penchant for telling stories. Several short stories were included, followed by the novels The Old Curiosity Shop and Barnaby Rudge.

The Haunted Man and the Ghost's Bargain

The Haunted Man and the Ghost's Bargain

by Charles Dickens

Gothic

The Haunted Man and the Ghost's Bargain: A Fancy for Christmas-Time is a novella by Charles Dickens first published. The Haunted Man and the Ghost's Bargain is the last of Dickens's five Christmas novellas, following A Christmas Carol, The Chimes, The Cricket on the Hearth and The Battle of Life.

Pictures from Italy

Pictures from Italy

by Charles Dickens

Travel

Pictures from Italy reveals the concerns of its author as he presents, according to Kate Flint, the country "like a chaotic magic-lantern show, fascinated both by the spectacle it offers, and by himself as spectator".

In The Chimes A Goblin Story of Some Bells That Rang an Old Year out and a New Year In, the Chimes: A Goblin Story of Some Bells that Rang an Old Year Out and a New Year In, commonly referred to as The Chimes, is a novella by Charles Dickens, first published, one year after A Christmas Carol. The Chimes A Goblin Story of Some Bells That Rang an Old Year out and a New Year In is the second in his series of "Christmas books", five novellas with strong social and moral messages that he published during the 1840s.

American Notes

by Charles Dickens

American Notes for General Circulation is a travelogue by Charles Dickens detailing his trip to North America. While there he acted as a critical observer of North American society, almost as if returning a status report on their progress.

by Charles Dickens

Oliver Twist

Oliver Twist

by Charles Dickens

Novel

Oliver Twist; or, The Parish Boy's Progress, is the second novel by English author Charles Dickens. The story stays with orphan Oliver Twist, who, after being raised in a workhouse, escapes to London, where he meets a gang of juvenile pickpockets led by the elderly criminal Fagin, discovers the secrets of his parentage, and reconnects with his remaining family.

Dombey and Son

Dombey and Son

by Charles Dickens

Novel

Dombey and Son stays with the fortunes of a shipping firm owner, who is frustrated at the lack of a son to follow him in his footsteps; he initially rejects his daughter's love before eventually becoming reconciled with her. Dombey and Son features many Dickensian themes, such as arranged marriages, child cruelty, betrayal, deceit, and relations between people from different British social classes.
