The Last of the Mohicans; A narrative of 1757

The Last of the Mohicans; A narrative of 1757

by James Fenimore Cooper

Adventure · History · Novel

The Last of the Mohicans; A narrative of 1757 is the second book of the Leatherstocking Tales pentalogy and the best known to contemporary audiences. The Pathfinder, published 14 years later, is its sequel; its prequel, The Deerslayer, was published a year after The Pathfinder.

The Pathfinder; Or, The Inland Sea

The Pathfinder; Or, The Inland Sea

by James Fenimore Cooper

Romance · Adventure · History

The Pathfinder; Or, The Inland Sea is the fourth novel Cooper wrote featuring Natty Bumppo, his fictitious frontier hero, and the third chronological episode of the Leatherstocking Tales. The inland sea of the title is Lake Ontario.

The Pioneers; Or, The Sources of the Susquehanna

The Pioneers; Or, The Sources of the Susquehanna

by James Fenimore Cooper

Adventure · History · Novel

The Pioneers; Or, The Sources of the Susquehanna was the first of five novels published which became known as the Leatherstocking Tales. The Pioneers is the fourth novel in terms of the chronology of the novels' plots.

The Lake Gun

The Lake Gun

by James Fenimore Cooper

Short stories · Fantasy

In The Lake Gun, the short story was commissioned by George E. Wood for $100, and published in a miscellany titled The Parthenon.

The Deerslayer

The Deerslayer

by James Fenimore Cooper

Adventure · History · Novel

The Deerslayer, or The First War-Path was James Fenimore Cooper's fifth and last novel published his Leatherstocking Tales. Its – time period makes it the first installment chronologically and in the lifetime of the hero of the Leatherstocking tales, Natty Bumppo.

The Prairie

The Prairie

by James Fenimore Cooper

Adventure · History · Novel

In The Prairie, his fictitious frontier hero Bumppo is never called by his name, but is instead referred to as "the trapper" or "the old man". Chronologically The Prairie is the fifth and final installment of the Leatherstocking Tales, though it was published before The Pathfinder and The Deerslayer.

The Pilot: A Tale of the Sea

The Pilot: A Tale of the Sea

by James Fenimore Cooper

Adventure · History · Novel

In The Pilot: A Tale of the Sea, the subject is the life of a naval pilot during the American Revolution. The Pilot: A Tale of the Sea is often considered the earliest example of nautical fiction in American literature.

Satanstoe; Or, the Littlepage Manuscripts. A Tale of the Colony

Satanstoe; Or, the Littlepage Manuscripts. A Tale of the Colony

by James Fenimore Cooper

History · Novel

Satanstoe; Or, the Littlepage Manuscripts. A Tale of the Colony, sometimes listed with the alternate title The Family of Littlepage or The Littlepage Manuscripts, is the first of a three novel cycle, followed by The Chainbearer and The Redskins. Satanstoe; Or, the Littlepage Manuscripts. A Tale of the Colony is a fictional autobiography which explores the 18th century colony of New York.

The Spy

The Spy

by James Fenimore Cooper

History · Novel

In The Spy, his second novel, it was published in by Wiley & Halsted. The plot is set during the American Revolution and was inspired in part by the family friend John Jay.

Wyandotté; Or, The Hutted Knoll: A Tale

Wyandotté; Or, The Hutted Knoll: A Tale

by James Fenimore Cooper

History · Novel

Wyandotté; Or, The Hutted Knoll: A Tale is set in New York state during the American Revolution. The main character of the novel is an Indian, "Saucy Nick", also called Wyandotté ("Great Chief"), whose depictions violate stereotypes of Native Americans.

The Headsman; Or, The Abbaye des Vignerons

The Headsman; Or, The Abbaye des Vignerons

by James Fenimore Cooper

Novel

The Headsman; Or, The Abbaye des Vignerons was inspired by one of Cooper's trips during his European travels. The Headsman; Or, The Abbaye des Vignerons is one of three of Cooper's "European" novels, following The Bravo and The Heidenmauer, all of which use the European setting to deal with socio-political contrast with American institutions.

Miles Wallingford Sequel to "Afloat and Ashore"

Miles Wallingford Sequel to "Afloat and Ashore"

by James Fenimore Cooper

Adventure · Novel

In Miles Wallingford Sequel to "Afloat and Ashore", Miles Wallingford: Sequel to Afloat and Ashore is a nautical novel written by American writer James Fenimore Cooper.

The Red Rover: A Tale

The Red Rover: A Tale

by James Fenimore Cooper

Adventure · Novel

The Red Rover: A Tale was originally published in Paris before being published in London three days later. The Red Rover: A Tale was not published in the United States in Philadelphia.
