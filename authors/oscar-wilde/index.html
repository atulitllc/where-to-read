The Ballad of Reading Gaol

by Oscar Wilde

1901 · Poetry

In The Ballad of Reading Gaol, Wilde had been incarcerated in Reading after being convicted of gross indecency with other men and sentenced to two years' hard labour in prison. During his imprisonment, on Tuesday, 7 July, a hanging took place.

Lady Windermere's Fan

Lady Windermere's Fan

by Oscar Wilde

Plays

Lady Windermere's Fan, A Play About a Good Woman is a four-act comedy by Oscar Wilde, first performed on Saturday, 20 February, at the St James's Theatre in London. Lady Windermere's Fan concerns Lady Windermere, who suspects that her husband is having an affair with another woman; she confronts him with it.

The Importance of Being Earnest: A Trivial Comedy for Serious People

The Importance of Being Earnest: A Trivial Comedy for Serious People

by Oscar Wilde

Plays

In The Importance of Being Earnest: A Trivial Comedy for Serious People, First performed on 14 February at the St James's Theatre in London, it is a farcical comedy depicting the tangled affairs of two young men about town who lead double lives to evade unwanted social obligations, both assuming the name Ernest while wooing the two young women of their affections.

A Woman of No Importance

A Woman of No Importance

by Oscar Wilde

Plays

A Woman of No Importance by Oscar Wilde is "a new and original play of modern life", in four acts, first given on 19 April at the Haymarket Theatre, London. Like Wilde's other society plays, it satirises English upper-class society.

A House of Pomegranates

A House of Pomegranates

by Oscar Wilde

Short stories · Fantasy · Children's books

A House of Pomegranates is Wilde's second fairy tale collection, following The Happy Prince and Other Tales. He said of the book that it was "intended neither for the British child nor the British public".

An ideal husband

An ideal husband

by Oscar Wilde

Plays

An ideal husband was first produced at the Haymarket Theatre, London and ran for 124 performances. An ideal husband has been revived in many theatre productions and adapted for the cinema, radio and television.

The Happy Prince, and Other Tales

The Happy Prince, and Other Tales

by Oscar Wilde

Short stories · Fantasy · Children's books

The Happy Prince, and Other Tales contains five stories that are highly popular among children and frequently read in schools: "The Happy Prince," "The Nightingale and the Rose," "The Selfish Giant," "The Devoted Friend," and "The Remarkable Rocket. " The short stories are valued for their morals, and have been made into animated films.

De Profundis

De Profundis

by Oscar Wilde

Letters

De Profundis (Latin: "from the depths") is a letter written by Oscar Wilde during his imprisonment in Reading Gaol, to his friend and lover Lord Alfred "Bosie" Douglas. In its first half, Wilde recounts their previous relationship and extravagant lifestyle which resulted eventually in Wilde's conviction and imprisonment for gross indecency.

The Soul of Man under Socialism

The Soul of Man under Socialism

by Oscar Wilde

Philosophy · Letters

In The Soul of Man under Socialism, the writing of "The Soul of Man" followed Wilde's conversion to anarchist philosophy, following his reading of the works of Peter Kropotkin. In "The Soul of Man" Wilde argues that, under capitalism, "the majority of people spoil their lives by an unhealthy and exaggerated altruism—are forced, indeed, so to spoil them": instead of realising their true talents, they waste their time solving the social problems caused by capitalism, without taking their common cause away.

A Florentine Tragedy; La Sainte Courtisane

A Florentine Tragedy; La Sainte Courtisane

by Oscar Wilde

Plays

In A Florentine Tragedy; La Sainte Courtisane, the subject concerns Simone, a wealthy 16th-century Florentine merchant who finds his wife Bianca in the arms of a local prince, Guido Bardi. After feigning hospitality, Simone challenges the interloper to a duel, disarms him, and strangles him.

The Picture of Dorian Gray

The Picture of Dorian Gray

by Oscar Wilde

Gothic · History · Novel

In The Picture of Dorian Gray, a shorter novella-length version was published in the July issue of the American periodical Lippincott's Monthly Magazine, while the novel-length version was published in April. As the only novel written by Wilde, it is widely regarded as a classic of both Gothic and English literature, having been adapted many times for films, stage, plays, and other forms of art performances.
